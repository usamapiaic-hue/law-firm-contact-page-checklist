# Law Firm Contact Page Checklist

The contact page is where a law firm website either produces a client or does not. People who reach it have already decided to get in touch; the page's only job is to make that easy and reassuring. This checklist covers what to put on the page and how to test it. Published by Legal Web Agency.

## Above the fold

| Element | Why |
|---------|-----|
| Phone number as a tappable link | Most legal enquiries on mobile are calls |
| One short sentence on what happens next | "An attorney or our intake team will call you back within one business day." |
| A short form (4-6 fields) | Long forms lose people; the details can wait for the call |
| Office hours and after-hours option | People with urgent legal problems call at night |

## The form

- **Fields:** name, phone, email, a short "how can we help?" box, and optionally the practice area. Ask for more on the first call, not on the form.
- **Labels:** visible labels, not just placeholder text.
- **Consent line:** a plain statement that sending the form does not create an attorney-client relationship, and that visitors should not send confidential details yet.
- **Confirmation:** a thank-you message or page that repeats when and how the firm will reply.
- **Spam:** a hidden honeypot field or invisible CAPTCHA, not a puzzle the visitor has to solve.

## Make sure the form actually reaches someone

This is the step most firms skip. A form can show "submitted", save the entry and still never alert anyone, for example when its notification is sent "from" a free email address the website's server is not allowed to send for, and the receiving mail server quietly drops it.

1. Set the notification's From address to an address on the firm's own domain.
2. Set Reply-To to the visitor's email field, so replying answers the client.
3. Send a test from a phone every week and time the response.
4. Compare the number of saved submissions with the number of alerts each month.

## Trust signals near the form

- Two or three short client reviews.
- Photos of the people who will answer.
- Office address with a map, if the firm sees clients in person.
- Languages spoken.

## Multiple ways in

Some people will not fill in a form. Offer at least two of: phone, text, form, booking link and chat. If a chat or text tool is used, label it honestly ("intake team", not "attorney") and check the state's advertising and solicitation rules.

## Measure it

Track calls and form submissions from the contact page separately, by source. A drop usually means something broke, not that demand fell.
